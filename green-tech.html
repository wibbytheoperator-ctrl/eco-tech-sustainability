Welcome to Green Technology!

Technology for a cleaner, greener and sustainable planet. Green technology uses science and innovation to create products and systems that protect the environment.

What is Green Technology?

Green technology, or green-tech, refers to the use of technology that reduces environmental impact and promotes sustainability.

Sustainability means protecting natural resources for future generations.

Solar Energy

Solar energy is power we get from the sun. Using solar panels, sunlight is converted into electricity.

How it works: Solar panels absorb sunlight and convert it to DC power. An inverter then changes it to AC power.

Advantages: Low maintenance, reduces electricity bills, and produces electricity without direct air pollution during operation.

Disadvantage: Electricity generation depends on sunlight, so production is lower or unavailable at night.

Wind Energy

Wind energy uses wind turbines to convert the kinetic energy of wind into electricity.

How it works: Wind spins the blades of a turbine, which drives a generator to produce electricity.

Advantages: Clean electricity generation, no fuel cost, and the ability to provide power to many households.

Disadvantage: Wind turbines can be noisy, require suitable locations, and depend on wind speed.

Hydropower

Hydropower is generated from flowing water, usually from rivers or dams. It is one of the most efficient renewable energy sources.

How it works: Flowing water turns turbines in a dam, generating electricity.

Advantages: High energy conversion efficiency, reliable electricity generation, and long operating lifespans.

Disadvantage: Expensive to build and can affect aquatic habitats and marine life.

Recycling

Recycling reduces waste and helps protect nature by allowing materials to be processed and reused instead of being discarded.

Key Takeaways

No single energy source is perfect. A balanced clean energy system can use all three sources together to support reliable electricity delivery.

How They Work Together

  • Solar: Provides electricity during daylight hours.
  • Wind: Generates electricity when wind conditions are suitable.
  • Hydro: Can provide reliable and flexible power to help balance changes in solar and wind generation.
