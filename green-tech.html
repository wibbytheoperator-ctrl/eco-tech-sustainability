Welcome to Green Tech!

Green technology uses science and innovation to create products and systems that protect the environment.

What is Green Technology?

Green technology or green-tech is the use of technology that reduces environmental impact and promotes sustainability.

Solar Energy

Solar energy is power we get from the sun. Using solar panels, sunlight is converted into electricity.

How it works: Solar panels absorb sunlight and convert it to DC power, then an inverter changes it to AC power.

Advantages: Low maintenance, reduces electricity bills, no pollution.

Disadvantage: Only works during daytime and needs sunlight.

Wind Energy

Wind energy uses wind turbines to convert the kinetic energy of wind into electricity.

How it works: Wind spins the blades of a turbine, which drives a generator to produce electricity.

Advantages: Clean, no fuel cost, can power many houses.

Disadvantage: Noisy, needs large space, and depends on wind speed.

Hydro Energy

Hydropower is generated from flowing water, usually from rivers or dams. It is one of the most efficient renewable energy sources.

How it works: Flowing water turns turbines in a dam, generating electricity.

Advantages: Very efficient (85-90%), provides constant power, long lifespan of 50-100 years.

Disadvantage: Expensive to build and can affect marine life.

Key Takeaways

No single energy source is perfect. A balanced clean energy system uses all three sources together to ensure stable power delivery.

How They Work Together

  • Solar: Provides peak power during hot daytime hours.
  • Wind: Generates strong power at night and during stormy weather.
  • Hydro: Acts as reliable backup power to balance solar and wind drops.
